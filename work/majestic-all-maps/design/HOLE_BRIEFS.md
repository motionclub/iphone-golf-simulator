# Per-hole design briefs (source of truth for every ref of a hole)

Every reference image of a hole is generated from **its brief below plus its course block**, so the tee, flyover, mid-fairway, green-back and off-line views agree with each other. The approved tee image of a hole is passed as a second reference to that hole's other views ("same place, same landmarks, same light").

Directions are relative to the line of play: **FWD** is toward the green, **BACK** toward the tee and behind it, and **L/R** are left and right of the line as seen from the tee.

Rules for all holes:
- The hole's routing, fairway shape, green, bunkers, water and lava, tee markers, the golfer and the gameplay hazards (floating RockGate/BasaltGate boulders, WindLift rings, LavaVents, IceRunway) **stay where they are and stay readable**.
- Only look, light, dressing, sky and atmosphere change.
- Floating in-world labels and UI are left out of the refs.
- The style is the house style from chat 9: stylized soft-plastic toy diorama (Wii Sports Resort / Mario Golf / Switch Sports), bright and readable, never realistic grit.
- **Every view has a subject:** each of the four directions gets a named focal point.
- **Dressing density:** dense and detailed within ~25 yd of the play corridor and the green, clustered groups (never even scatter) out to ~80 yd, then a far layer of big shapes (landforms, sea life, sky). There are no flat empty plates.
- **Island edges are never a bare bevel.** Each gets a shore band (sand or rock shelf), turquoise shallows with foam rings, or a cliff with surf.

## Course A — Cliffside (holes 7, 8, 9, 12, 15): "Morning on the Emerald Coast"
- **Hour:** fresh morning sea light, sun from the right/behind the golfer, long soft shadows, crisp air with light blue aerial haze.
- **Palette:** emerald and lime turf with clear mow stripes; warm cream limestone; white lighthouse with red bands; turquoise shallows → deep blue sea; coral bougainvillea accents; white sails.
- **Course landmark (seen from every hole):** a **white-and-red lighthouse on a headland**. Chat 9's rule: one landmark visible from every tee.
- **Far layer:** chat 2's karst islands as layered landforms fading into haze.
- **Life:** sailboats, gulls, a breaching whale (12), hot-air balloons, kites.
- **Hazard rocks:** mossy limestone boulders with ivy and small flowers.
- **Weather:** Sea Fog rolls in low over the water and fills the inlets; the green and the ball stay clear. Rain clears to a rainbow over the bay. Thunderstorm rolls in from the sea with lightning over the far islands. Gusty shows whitecaps, leaning palms and grass, kite strings and blowing flags.

## Course B — Wild Isles (holes 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 20): "An adventure archipelago, golden afternoon"
- **Hour:** warm golden afternoon, sun low from the left, honey light, long shadows.
- **Identity:** each hole is its own isle with its own biome, framed by sea and the karst ring. That distinguishes the course from Cliffside, where every hole shares one coast.
- **Course landmark (seen across the course):** a **giant twin waterfall** pouring off a high central isle, with a ruined temple on top. It's on 19 and visible in the distance from the others; a misty glow marks it.
- **Life:** flocks of birds, butterflies and falling petals or leaves on the wind (pollen already exists on 19/20), windmill sails turning.
- **Hazard rocks:** per biome: mossy granite (13/14), ice-glazed rock (17), red sandstone (18), carved temple stone (19), flower-covered boulders (20).
- **Weather:** Rain arrives as a warm shower with sunbeams through it. Thunderstorm brings dark clouds over the waterfall isle with lightning far off. Sea Fog is low mist in the valleys and on the lagoon. Gusty sends leaf and petal swirls, bending grass waves and racing windmill sails. On 17 these become Snow (big soft flakes, gathered on branches), Blizzard (sideways snow, low sun as a white disc) and Freezing Fog (ice-crystal sparkle).

## Course C — Magma Open (holes 10, 16, 21, 22, 23): "Fire at dawn"
- **Hour:** blue-violet pre-dawn sky warming to orange at the horizon. Lava glow lights the rocks from below; the sun is a low warm disc on the far side.
- **Palette:** obsidian and basalt black-violet, molten orange-gold lava, ember red, vivid emerald turf (kept for readability), cool ash-grey clouds.
- **Course landmark:** the **great caldera volcano** with a glowing crater and a slow smoke plume, visible from every hole. 16 sits right under it.
- **Holes 10 and 16 (sea backdrop):** a volcanic archipelago with a dark sea reflecting the glow and smoking islets. **Holes 21–23 (lava lake):** the caldera floor. The lava disc's edge is hidden by **caldera walls** with lava falls, so the rim seam disappears.
- **Life:** ember drifts on the wind, lava bubbles and small fountains, steam vents, bats or ash birds.
- **Hazard rocks:** cracked basalt with glowing seams (BasaltGate); LavaVents as glowing fumaroles.
- **Weather:** Ashfall is soft grey flakes, the sky dims and lava glows brighter. Eruption: the caldera erupts with an orange flash, lava bombs arcing far off and a rumble. Volcanic Smoke is low drifting sulphur-gold smoke with the lava glowing through it. Gusty sends embers streaming sideways and smoke plumes bending. *(10 and 16 draw sea weather today: rain and thunderstorm. PLAN decides whether they switch to ash; the refs show them in the ash family.)*
